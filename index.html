Livello A1

La mia grammatica

A pocket grammar for your first months of Italian. Each chapter is short: read the rule, look at the table, say the examples out loud. Finish with the practice drill at the end.

Capitolo 1

Come si legge

Italian is read the way it is written. Every vowel is pronounced, and a few letter groups are worth learning on day one.

LettersSounds likeExample
c + e, i"ch" in churchcena, ciao
ch + e, i"k" in kiteche, chiave
g + e, i"j" in jamgelato, giorno
gh + e, i"g" in gospaghetti, ghiaccio
gli"lli" in millionfiglio, moglie
gn"ny" in canyonbagno, gnocchi
sc + e, i"sh" in shoppesce, uscire

double lettershold them a little longerpala ≠ palla, nono ≠ nonno

The accent mark shows the stressed final vowel: città, perché, così. It also changes meaning: e = and, è = is.

Capitolo 2

I pronomi soggetto

Every verb table in this book follows the same six people. Italians usually drop the pronoun because the verb ending already says who it is: parlo already means "I speak".

ItalianoEnglish

Tu o Lei?

Use tu with friends, family, children and people your age in casual settings. Use Lei (capital L, the formal "you") with shop staff, waiters, older people and anyone you don't know. Lei takes the same verb form as lui / lei.

Come stai? How are you? (to a friend)

Come sta? How are you? (formal)

Capitolo 3

Essere e avere

The two most important verbs. Both are irregular, so learn them by heart first. You will also need them later to build the past tense.

Italian "has" where English "is"

Many feelings use avere in Italian:

avere fameto be hungry
avere seteto be thirsty
avere caldo / freddoto be hot / cold
avere sonnoto be sleepy
avere paurato be afraid
avere ragioneto be right
avere ... annito be ... years old

C'è e ci sono

C'è = there is, ci sono = there are. C'è un bar qui vicino? Ci sono due camere.

Capitolo 4

Verbi regolari

Italian verbs end in -are, -ere or -ire. To conjugate a regular verb, remove the ending and add the endings below. Most new verbs you meet will be -are verbs.

Notice that io always ends in -o, tu in -i and noi in -iamo. That is already half the work.

Esempi

Two spelling rules

Some -ire verbs add -isc- like capire: finire (to finish), preferire (to prefer), pulire (to clean).

Capitolo 5

Verbi irregolari

These are the irregular verbs you will hear every day. Learn them a few at a time. Volere, potere, dovere are followed directly by an infinitive: voglio mangiare, posso entrare, devo andare.

Capitolo 6

Verbi riflessivi

A reflexive verb is something you do to yourself. In the dictionary it ends in -si. Put the small pronoun (mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si) in front of the verb.

Others in the same pattern: lavarsi (to wash), vestirsi (to get dressed), svegliarsi (to wake up), divertirsi (to have fun).

Capitolo 7

Piacere: mi piace

Italian says "it pleases me" instead of "I like it". The verb agrees with the thing you like, not with you.

One thing / a verbSeveral things
I likemi piace la pizzami piacciono gli spaghetti
you liketi piace ballareti piacciono i cani
he / she likesgli / le piace il maregli / le piacciono i film
we likeci piace Romaci piacciono le lasagne

Ti piace il caffè? Sì, mi piace molto. Do you like coffee? Yes, I like it a lot.

Non mi piacciono i funghi. I don't like mushrooms.

Capitolo 8

Dire di no e fare domande

Negazione

Put non in front of the verb. That's it.

Parlo tedesco. → Non parlo tedesco. I speak German. → I don't speak German.

Non mangio mai la carne. I never eat meat. (Italian keeps both words)

Domande

A question is often just a statement with your voice going up at the end. The word order does not change.

Sei stanca. You're tired.

Sei stanca? Are you tired?

Question words: chi (who), che cosa (what), dove (where), quando (when), perché (why), come (how), quanto (how much), quale (which).

Capitolo 9

Articoli e nomi

Every noun is masculine or feminine. Most masculine nouns end in -o (plural -i), most feminine nouns end in -a (plural -e). Nouns ending in -e can be either, and become -i in the plural.

the (singular)the (plural)a / an
masculineil libroi libriun libro
masc. before vowell'amicogli amiciun amico
masc. before s+consonant, zlo zainogli zainiuno zaino
femininela casale caseuna casa
fem. before vowell'amicale amicheun'amica

Adjectives match the noun: un ragazzo alto, una ragazza alta, due ragazzi alti, due ragazze alte.

Capitolo 10

Il passato prossimo

The everyday past tense ("I ate", "I have eaten"). It has two parts: the present of avere or essere + the past participle.

InfinitiveParticipleExample
parlareparlatoHo parlato con Marco.
venderevendutoAbbiamo venduto la macchina.
dormiredormitoHai dormito bene?

Avere or essere?

Most verbs use avere. Verbs of movement and change (andare, venire, arrivare, partire, uscire, tornare, restare, nascere) and reflexive verbs use essere. With essere, the participle changes like an adjective: -o, -a, -i, -e.

Irregular participles to know

Ieri sono andata a Roma e ho visto il Colosseo. Yesterday I went to Rome and saw the Colosseum.

Capitolo 11

Pratica: coniuga!

Write the correct present-tense form. Accents count, but you can type e' for è or puo' for può. For reflexive verbs, include the little pronoun: mi chiamo.
